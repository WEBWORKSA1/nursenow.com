Licensing & registration

Get licensed — the real sequence, step by step

Whether you graduated in Texas, Toronto or Manila, this is the order things actually happen in — with links to the official bodies that decide.

United States: licensure by examination

  1. Graduate from an approved RN (ADN/BSN) or PN program.
  2. Apply to your state board of nursing for licensure — fingerprints, background check and transcripts are typical.
  3. Register with Pearson VUE for the NCLEX-RN or NCLEX-PN and pay the registration fee.
  4. Receive your Authorization to Test (ATT) and schedule within its validity window.
  5. Pass the NCLEX; your board issues the license and it appears on Nursys.

Already licensed in another state? You apply for licensure by endorsement with the new state’s board — unless you hold a multistate compact license that already covers it.

The Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC)

The NLC lets RNs and LPN/VNs whose primary state of residence is a compact state hold one multistate license and practise in other compact states. More than 40 US jurisdictions have enacted it, though several large states — including California and New York — have not joined as of this writing. Membership changes, so always check the live map at nursecompact.com before you relocate or accept a travel contract. A separate APRN Compact for nurse practitioners has been adopted by some states but requires a minimum number of states before it takes effect.

Canada: provincial registration

Nursing is regulated provincially. Canadian graduates apply to the regulator where they want to work and write the entry-to-practice exam:

  • Registered nurses (outside Québec) write the NCLEX-RN.
  • Practical nurses (LPN; RPN in Ontario) write the REx-PN or CPNRE, depending on the province.
  • Québec is separate: the Ordre des infirmières et infirmiers du Québec (OIIQ) runs its own professional exam, and French proficiency is required to practise.
  • Many regulators also require a jurisprudence assessment on the province’s laws and standards — Ontario’s CNO, for example.

Internationally educated nurses (IENs)

Canada (outside Québec)

  1. Start with NNAS — the National Nursing Assessment Service verifies your education, registration and work history and produces an advisory report. NNAS does not license you.
  2. Apply to the provincial regulator (e.g., CNO in Ontario, BCCNM in BC). They assess your file and tell you what’s missing.
  3. Prove language proficiency with an approved test (such as IELTS Academic or CELBAN) if required.
  4. Close any gaps — a competency assessment or bridging program may be required.
  5. Write the registration exam (NCLEX-RN, REx-PN or CPNRE) and complete any jurisprudence assessment.

Immigration is a separate track (work permit or permanent residence). Licensing and immigration run in parallel — neither guarantees the other. Read the full Canada IEN guide.

United States

IENs typically need a credentials evaluation (for example, through CGFNS International), state board approval, the NCLEX and, for immigration, a VisaScreen certificate and an English test. Requirements vary by state.

Renewal & continuing education

Licenses renew on cycles set by each board or regulator. Many US states require a set number of continuing education contact hours; Canadian regulators typically require annual renewal plus a quality-assurance or continuing-competence program (self-assessment and a learning plan). Licensing FAQ

Is a nursing license valid in every US state?

No. Licenses are issued by individual state boards. A multistate license under the Nurse Licensure Compact lets you practise in other compact states; otherwise you apply for licensure by endorsement in each state.

Do Canadian nurses write the NCLEX?

RN applicants in every province except Québec write the NCLEX-RN. Practical nurses write the REx-PN or CPNRE depending on the province. Québec runs its own OIIQ professional exam.

Where do internationally educated nurses start in Canada?

Outside Québec, most internationally educated nurses start with the National Nursing Assessment Service (NNAS), then apply to the regulator in the province where they want to work.

How do I verify a nurse’s license?

In the US, use Nursys (nursys.com) or the state board’s lookup. In Canada, each provincial regulator publishes a public register.

Licensing rules, fees and timelines change. This page is general information, not legal or immigration advice — your regulator’s website is the final word.
