Leaving the bedside doesn’t mean leaving nursing. Your assessment skills, clinical knowledge and communication are exactly what insurers, health systems, tech companies and educators need. Here are twelve paths and what it takes to get in.

[[toc]]

## What remote employers expect

Most remote nursing roles want:

- **Two to five years of clinical experience**, often in acute care.
- **An active, unencumbered licence** — and for roles serving patients in many states, a **multistate compact licence** or licences in several states, because licensure follows the patient. See [the compact explained](/blog/nurse-licensure-compact-explained/).
- **Strong documentation and computer skills.**
- A quiet, private home workspace that meets privacy requirements.

## 12 non-bedside paths

1. **Telephone triage / telehealth nurse** — assess symptoms by phone or video using protocols and direct patients to the right level of care.
2. **Case manager** — coordinate care, discharge planning and resources; the CCM credential is valued.
3. **Utilization review nurse** — review medical necessity for insurers or hospitals.
4. **Clinical documentation integrity (CDI) specialist** — ensure documentation accurately reflects patient acuity.
5. **Nursing informatics** — build and optimise EHR workflows; bridges clinical care and technology.
6. **Nurse educator** — academic or clinical staff education; usually needs a graduate degree for academic roles.
7. **Quality and patient safety** — audits, data and improvement projects.
8. **Infection prevention** — surveillance, education and outbreak management.
9. **Legal nurse consultant** — review records for law firms and insurers.
10. **Clinical research nurse** — coordinate trials, consent and protocol adherence.
11. **Health content and medical writing** — patient education, exam questions and clinical content.
12. **Sales, product and clinical specialist roles** — for device, pharma and health-tech companies.

## How to make the switch

- **Pick one target role** and read 20 postings. List the repeated requirements.
- **Close gaps** with a certification, short course or internal project (join a unit committee on quality, documentation or education).
- **Rewrite your resume** around transferable skills — documentation accuracy, patient education, coordination. Start with the [resume builder](/tools/resume-builder/).
- **Network:** many non-bedside hires come through internal transfers and referrals.
- **Consider a hybrid step** — a clinic, PACU or day-shift role — while you build experience.

## Watch for remote-job scams

Legitimate employers don’t charge fees to apply, ask you to buy equipment upfront with your own money for reimbursement later, or interview only by text chat. Verify the company and recruiter independently. Browse vetted openings on our [jobs page](/jobs/?q=remote).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can new grad nurses get remote jobs?
It’s uncommon. Most remote roles require several years of clinical experience because you’ll work independently.

### Do remote nurses earn less than bedside nurses?
It varies. Some remote roles pay less than bedside work with differentials and overtime, while informatics, CDI and some case-management roles can pay comparably or more.

### Do I need a compact licence for remote nursing?
Often. Licensure is based on where the patient is located, so employers serving many states prefer nurses with a multistate licence.
