Since April 2023, the NCLEX has measured something it always cared about but couldn’t score well: **clinical judgment**. The result is the Next Generation NCLEX (NGN) — new item types, unfolding case studies and partial-credit scoring. The good news: once you understand the structure, NGN items are often more logical than old-style questions.

## The clinical judgment model behind every case study

NGN items are built on NCSBN’s Clinical Judgment Measurement Model. Each case study walks you through six steps:

1. **Recognise cues** — which findings in the record matter?
2. **Analyse cues** — what could those findings mean?
3. **Prioritise hypotheses** — which explanation is most likely or most urgent?
4. **Generate solutions** — what outcomes and interventions fit?
5. **Take action** — which interventions do you implement now?
6. **Evaluate outcomes** — did the client improve, stay the same or decline?

Case studies present a client record (nurses’ notes, vital signs, labs, orders) that updates as the scenario unfolds, usually with six questions per case. Standalone NGN items — bow-tie and trend — appear outside case studies.

## Item types and how to beat them

### Extended multiple response (select all that apply / select N)
You may be asked to select all that apply, a specific number of options, or options within grouped categories. **Strategy:** treat every option as an independent true/false statement about *this* client. Don’t hunt for a “typical” number of correct answers.

### Extended drag-and-drop
Move options into categories, blanks or a sequence. **Strategy:** for sequencing, place the first and last steps first, then fill the middle. For categories, sort the options you’re sure of before tackling uncertain ones.

### Drop-down (cloze) and drop-down rationale
Complete a sentence or table from drop-down lists — sometimes a “because” statement linking a condition to its cause. **Strategy:** read the whole sentence before choosing; later blanks usually constrain earlier ones.

### Enhanced hot spot (highlight)
Highlight words or phrases in a note or table. **Strategy:** highlight only what answers the question — for example, findings that *require follow-up* — not everything abnormal in history.

### Matrix / grid
Each row is a finding or action; columns are choices such as “indicated / not indicated / non-essential”. **Strategy:** work row by row and don’t let one row influence another.

### Bow-tie
A diagram with the condition in the centre, two actions to take on the left and two parameters to monitor on the right. **Strategy:** identify the condition first from the cues — the actions and monitoring parameters follow logically from it.

### Trend
A standalone item showing data across time (vital signs, intake/output, labs). **Strategy:** look for direction, not single values — a rising heart rate with falling blood pressure tells a story a single reading can’t.

## How partial credit works

Many NGN items use polytomous scoring:

- **0/1 scoring:** one point per correct response, no penalty for wrong ones (common in some drag-and-drop and grid items).
- **+/− scoring:** a point for each correct selection and a point deducted for each incorrect one, with a minimum of zero (common in select-all-that-apply). Guessing wildly costs you.
- **Rationale scoring:** paired responses must both be correct to earn the point.

The takeaway: on select-all-that-apply items, choose only what you’re confident is correct for this client.

## A practice routine for NGN

- Do two case studies a day once your content review is underway.
- After each case, write one sentence: *What was the condition, and which cue proved it?*
- Track which step of the model you miss most — many candidates struggle with “prioritise hypotheses”.
- Practise reading a full client record in under two minutes, flagging abnormal values and trends.

Pair this with daily standalone practice on the [NurseNow NCLEX hub](/nclex/) and the [60-day study plan](/blog/how-to-pass-the-nclex-first-try/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many case studies are on the NCLEX?
Candidates typically see several unfolding case studies of six items each as part of the 85–150 total items, plus standalone NGN items. NCSBN publishes the current structure on NCLEX.com.

### Do NGN questions count more than traditional questions?
NGN items can award multiple points through partial-credit scoring, but every item feeds the same adaptive ability estimate. Treat each item as important.

### Are NGN questions harder?
They’re more complex, not necessarily harder. Because they reward reasoning step by step, candidates who practise the clinical judgment model often find them more predictable than older items.
