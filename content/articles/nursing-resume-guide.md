Hospital recruiters skim, and many health systems screen applications with an **applicant tracking system (ATS)** before anyone reads them. A nursing resume has one job: prove — quickly and in the employer’s own language — that you can safely do the role. Here’s how.

[[toc]]

## The structure that works

1. **Header:** name with credentials (e.g., *Jordan Lee, RN, BScN*), city/province or state, phone, email, optional LinkedIn.
2. **Professional summary:** two or three lines naming your licence, years of experience, specialty and one standout strength.
3. **Licences:** licence type, jurisdiction and status (e.g., *Registered Nurse — Texas Board of Nursing, multistate, active*). Add the licence number only if the posting asks.
4. **Certifications:** BLS, ACLS, PALS, NRP, TNCC, CCRN and others, with expiry dates.
5. **Clinical experience:** reverse chronological, with unit type, bed count or acuity, and bullet achievements.
6. **Education:** degree, school, year. New grads add clinical rotations and capstone/preceptorship.
7. **Skills:** EHR systems, procedures and equipment relevant to the role.

One page suits most nurses with under ten years of experience. Two pages are fine for experienced nurses, educators and leaders.

## Beat the ATS with the employer’s own words

ATS software ranks resumes by how closely they match the posting. Copy the posting into a document and highlight:

- **Unit and specialty terms** — “telemetry”, “post-anesthesia care”, “Level I trauma”.
- **Skills and equipment** — “titratable drips”, “CRRT”, “Epic”, “central lines”.
- **Certifications** — written exactly as the posting does (“ACLS” *and* “Advanced Cardiac Life Support” if space allows).

Then use those exact terms naturally in your summary, bullets and skills. Our [resume builder](/tools/resume-builder/) suggests keywords for eight specialties with one tap.

Formatting rules for ATS: a simple single-column layout, standard section headings, no text inside images or text boxes, and common fonts. Submit a Word file if the posting requests it; otherwise a clean PDF is usually fine.

## Bullet formula: action + context + result

Weak: *Responsible for patient care.*

Strong: *Managed care for 5–6 telemetry patients per shift on a 32-bed cardiac step-down unit, maintaining zero falls over 12 months.*

Use numbers where you can — ratios, bed counts, patients precepted, audit scores, project outcomes. Examples:

- *Precepted 6 new graduate nurses through 12-week orientation.*
- *Served as relief charge nurse for a 28-bed medical unit, coordinating assignments for 7 staff.*
- *Led unit hand-hygiene audit; compliance rose from 78% to 94% in one quarter.*
- *Titrated vasoactive and sedation infusions for ventilated ICU patients.*

## New-grad resumes

No paid nursing experience yet? Lead with what you have:

- **Preceptorship/capstone:** unit, hours and responsibilities (“carried a 4-patient assignment under preceptor supervision”).
- **Clinical rotations:** list settings and notable skills.
- **Related work:** nursing assistant, PSW, EMT, unit clerk — healthcare exposure counts.
- **Awards, leadership, languages:** bilingual skills are valuable in many regions.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Listing duties instead of results.
- Leaving out licence jurisdiction or certification expiry dates.
- Including patient-identifying details in examples (never do this).
- One generic resume for every job — tailor the summary and top bullets each time.
- Typos. Read it aloud, then have someone else read it.

## Cover letters: short and specific

Three short paragraphs: why this unit and employer, the two strongest matches between your experience and the posting, and a confident close. Mention a detail that shows you researched them — a program, a value, a recent expansion.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I include my nursing licence number on my resume?
It’s usually optional. Listing licence type, jurisdiction and status is enough for most applications; employers verify through Nursys or the provincial register. Include the number if the posting requests it.

### How long should a nursing resume be?
One page for most new graduates and nurses with under ten years of experience; two pages for experienced nurses, educators, leaders and advanced practice nurses.

### Do I need a different resume for travel nursing?
Yes — emphasise adaptability: number of facilities, EHR systems used, float experience and the specific units and charting systems you can work in on day one.
