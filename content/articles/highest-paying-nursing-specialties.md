“Highest-paying nursing jobs” lists are everywhere, and many quote numbers with no source. Here we stick to official US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) data, explain what the medians mean, and focus on the levers you control.

[[toc]]

## Nurse pay by role (US, BLS May 2024)

| Role | Median annual pay | Typical education |
|---|---|---|
| Nurse anesthetist (CRNA) | $223,210 | Doctoral nurse anesthesia program |
| Nurse practitioner (NP) | $129,210 | Master’s or doctorate (NP) |
| Nurse midwife (CNM) | $128,790 | Master’s or doctorate (midwifery) |
| Registered nurse (RN) | $93,600 | ADN or BSN + NCLEX-RN |
| Licensed practical/vocational nurse | $62,340 | Certificate/diploma + NCLEX-PN |
| Nursing assistant | $39,530 | Certificate + state competency exam |

*Source: BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics, May 2024. The BLS also reports a combined median of $132,050 for nurse anesthetists, midwives and practitioners as a group.*

A **median** means half of workers earn more and half earn less. Your actual pay depends on location, setting, experience, shift and union status.

## Why CRNAs and NPs earn more

Advanced practice roles add graduate education, national certification and an expanded scope — anesthesia delivery, diagnosis, prescribing. The BLS projects strong growth for these roles through 2034, which supports demand. The trade-off is time and cost: a CRNA program is doctoral-level and typically requires critical-care experience first.

## The levers that raise RN pay — without a new degree

1. **Location.** State and metro area are the single biggest factor. California posts the highest average RN wages in BLS data, but cost of living eats into the difference — compare take-home against rent.
2. **Setting.** Hospitals, outpatient surgery and government roles often pay differently from long-term care and clinics.
3. **Shift differentials.** Nights, evenings and weekends add up. Model yours in the [pay calculator](/tools/pay-calculator/).
4. **Specialty and certification.** Critical care, OR, L&D and emergency roles often carry premiums; many employers pay a [certification differential](/blog/nursing-certifications-that-raise-your-pay/).
5. **Overtime and incentive shifts.** Lucrative, but watch fatigue — see our [night shift guide](/blog/night-shift-survival-guide/).
6. **Travel and contract work.** Short-term contracts can pay well; understand stipends and tax rules first in [Travel Nursing 101](/blog/travel-nursing-101/).
7. **Negotiation.** Many nurses never ask. Bring market data and your certifications to the conversation.

## Specialties nurses often cite for higher pay

Pay data by bedside specialty is less standardised than by occupation, but roles that commonly carry higher rates or premiums include critical care, operating room, labor and delivery, emergency, cath lab and interventional radiology, and nurse leadership. Non-bedside paths such as informatics and case management can also pay well — see [remote and non-bedside nursing jobs](/blog/remote-and-non-bedside-nursing-jobs/).

## How nurse pay works in Canada

Most Canadian nurses work in publicly funded systems where wages are set by **provincial collective agreements**. Pay is a grid: your step increases with years of experience, and premiums apply for shifts, weekends, responsibility (such as charge duties) and sometimes education. The best reference is your union’s current agreement; [Job Bank Canada](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/) also publishes wage ranges by region for registered nurses (NOC 31301) and licensed practical nurses (NOC 32101).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the highest-paying nursing job?
In BLS data, nurse anesthetists (CRNAs) have the highest median pay of any nursing occupation — $223,210 in May 2024.

### How much does a registered nurse make in the US?
The BLS reports a median annual wage of $93,600 for registered nurses (May 2024). Pay varies widely by state, setting and experience.

### Do night shift nurses make more?
Usually, yes. Most employers pay a night differential per hour, which can add thousands of dollars a year. Check your employer’s policy or collective agreement.

### Is it worth becoming an NP for the money?
For many nurses, yes — but weigh tuition, lost income during school and the job market in your area. Compare the long-term earnings difference against the cost using real local salary data.
