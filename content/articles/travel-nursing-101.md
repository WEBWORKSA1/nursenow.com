Travel nursing pairs short-term contracts with higher-than-staff pay and a chance to see new places. It also comes with paperwork, tax rules and contracts that reward careful reading. This guide covers what you need before your first assignment.

[[toc]]

## Who can travel

Most agencies look for at least **one to two years of recent experience** in your specialty, because travellers are expected to work independently after a short orientation — often a single shift. Specialties in steady demand include med-surg/telemetry, ICU, emergency, OR, L&D and step-down.

## How contracts work

- **Length:** commonly 13 weeks, with options to extend.
- **Guaranteed hours:** usually 36–48 per week. Check what happens if the facility cancels shifts.
- **Shift and float:** confirm days vs nights, float units and whether you can be floated outside your competency.
- **Cancellation terms:** both sides can cancel; read the notice period and penalties.

## Understanding the pay package

A travel offer is usually a **blend**:

1. **Taxable hourly wage** — often lower than you’d expect.
2. **Stipends** for housing and meals & incidentals — which may be **non-taxable** if you qualify.
3. **Bonuses** — sign-on, completion or referral.
4. **Benefits** — health insurance, 401(k), licence and travel reimbursement.

Compare offers by **weekly gross** and **total contract value**, not the hourly rate alone. Our [pay calculator](/tools/pay-calculator/) compares two offers side by side.

## Tax home: the rule that makes stipends work

In the US, tax-free stipends are generally tied to maintaining a **tax home** — a regular place of residence and work where you have ongoing expenses — while you travel away from it and incur duplicate living costs. If you don’t maintain a tax home, stipends may be taxable. Rules are detailed and audits happen, so keep records and talk to a tax professional who works with travel nurses before you sign.

## Licensing for travel

- A **multistate compact licence** lets you take assignments in compact states without new licences — see [the compact explained](/blog/nurse-licensure-compact-explained/).
- Non-compact states require their own licence; some issue temporary licences faster. Many agencies reimburse licence costs.
- Keep BLS, ACLS and specialty certifications current — track them in the [licence tracker](/tools/license-tracker/).

## Choosing an agency and recruiter

Your recruiter shapes your experience. Look for:

- transparent pay breakdowns in writing before you submit;
- clear answers about guaranteed hours, cancellation and float policy;
- benefits from day one (or a clear waiting period);
- references from other travellers — community sites like The Gypsy Nurse publish agency reviews.

Working with two or three agencies lets you compare offers for the same job.

## Contract red flags

- Pay details that change after you’ve been submitted.
- No guaranteed hours, or a “flex” clause that lets the facility cancel many shifts.
- Vague float language (“other units as needed”).
- Pressure to sign the same day.
- Requests for personal financial information before you have a written offer.

## Housing

Take the agency-provided housing or a stipend and find your own. Many travellers use furnished monthly rentals; never wire a deposit for a place you haven’t verified.

## Travel nursing in Canada

Canada has agency and contract nursing too — including short-term contracts in rural, northern and remote communities, often with travel and accommodation covered. Licensure is provincial, so you need registration in each province where you work. Some provinces have moved to limit agency use in public systems, so check current rules where you want to work.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much experience do I need to become a travel nurse?
Most agencies want one to two years of recent experience in your specialty. High-acuity units may expect more.

### Are travel nurse stipends always tax-free?
No. In the US, stipends are generally non-taxable only if you maintain a qualifying tax home and incur duplicate expenses. Talk to a tax professional.

### Can I travel with a single-state licence?
Yes, but you’ll need a licence in each non-compact state you work in. A compact licence simplifies work across member states.
